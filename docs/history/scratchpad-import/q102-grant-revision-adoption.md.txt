## Q-102A — Upgrade candidates and suggestions

**AGREED at advisory-rule level.** The user said “proceed” after the recommended
shape and its core-philosophy check. Previous status: USER-PROPOSED; recommended
shape. Expose assignments using older adopted
revisions for which newer revisions are available, so administrators can review
and explicitly adopt appropriate changes. Keep suggestions available as the
assignment/revision state changes; no automatic adoption or notification
delivery/cadence is prescribed.

The view can show the assignment and recipient, adopted revision, available
revision, and the authority differences that need review. This is a conceptual
view, not approval of new JSON fields, an API, or an external notification system.

**Recommendation:** yes, as an advisory administration view separate from
effective authorization. “Older revision” means an update candidate, not invalid
authority and not proof that adopting the newest revision is appropriate.
Show permission/scope differences, not merely an unconditional upgrade prompt.

**Core-philosophy check and rationale:**

- Visibility helps administrators find adoption work without silently changing
  recipients' authority. Suggesting does not authorize or perform adoption.
- Any adoption still requires current administrative permission, valid parent
  support, and complete boundary validation. Listing a revision does not prove
  it is adoptable, especially if conditions changed after the list was built.
- Older adopted revisions remain subject to normal runtime validity and current
  parent ceilings; a newer publication alone does not invalidate them.
- Disabled grants/assignments cannot be enabled as a side effect of an upgrade
  suggestion or adoption. The Q-101 enablement rules remain separate.
- Auth can provide the authorized revision/assignment information; an admin
  interface or management layer can present ongoing suggestions. An optional
  suggestion feature must not become a runtime authorization dependency.
- A newer revision can narrow, broaden, or otherwise change authority. Newer
  does not automatically mean safer, required, or suitable for every recipient.

Parent-revision selection for dependent grants remains the next unresolved core
contract; adding an advisory view does not resolve or bypass it.

## The policy question

G1 is assigned to Team1 and TeamX. Assume valid upstream authority and all
unrelated conditions. Its current proposed revision 1 supplies FIN read; a new
proposed revision 2 supplies FIN read/write. The example does not authorize
publishing or adopting broader authority without the required validation.

**Q-102:** can Team1's assignment explicitly adopt revision 2 while TeamX's
assignment stays on revision 1, with the adoption authorized and boundary-validated?

| Assignment | Before | Proposed independently adopted state |
|---|---|---|
| G1 to Team1 | Revision 1: FIN read | Revision 2: FIN read/write |
| G1 to TeamX | Revision 1: FIN read | Revision 1: FIN read |

**Proposal:** revision adoption is controlled per assignment. Publishing a new
grant revision does not silently move existing assignments to it.

**Recommended answer: yes, with the following mandatory safeguards.** This is
a conditional fit with the existing philosophy, not approval of all revision
mechanics or evidence of a tested implementation.

| Core principle | Assessment of the recommendation |
|---|---|
| No silent authority expansion | Publication alone does not update an assignment; adoption is explicit. |
| Child authority remains within current parent authority | An old adopted revision must not preserve access that its required live supporting authority no longer permits. Revision pinning is not frozen independent authority. |
| Administration is separate from use | Adoption requires administrative-operation authorization and validation of complete resulting authority under Q-100. |
| Assignment, grant enablement, and effectiveness are separate | Revision selection must not bypass grant-wide disable/delete or assignment-specific disablement. |
| Four-part binding stays valid | Adoption must respect current team/grant support and inherited boundaries; it cannot bypass Q-101 structural guards. |
| Keep the model simple | Per-assignment adoption adds selection state and validation complexity; this cost must be explicit rather than treated as free. |

**Remaining design risk:** identify the parent revision that supplies a child
grant's ceiling when different assignments adopt different revisions. Without
that contract, we cannot claim deterministic, non-expanding lineage resolution
for every revision combination. This must be settled before finalizing the
combined revision schema; do not silently select latest or any broader holding.

**Rationale:** authoring reusable authority and changing a recipient's effective
authority are distinct operations. Independent adoption lets recipients retain
their explicitly approved version rather than coupling every assignment to a
single update. The trade-off is multiple coexisting revisions and additional
revision-selection validation.

This is a policy question, not an approved JSON format. No `grant_revision` or
other new field is adopted here. If the policy is approved, present exact
versioned grant/assignment excerpts for review before canonicalizing fields.
Parent revision selection, dependent-grant effects, identity-wide disable/delete,
and safe mutation mechanics must remain coherent with Q-101; this proposal does
not settle them or create an exception to current authority boundaries.
