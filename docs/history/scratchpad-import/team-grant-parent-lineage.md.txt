## Rule

A grant assigned to a child team must derive from one selected grant validly
assigned to that team's parent. This connects team hierarchy to authority lineage.

- Child permissions must be a subset of the parent's effective permissions.
- Effective child scope is parent scope AND additional child constraints.
- Preserve the specific supporting parent assignment, not only the reusable
  parent grant definition's ID.
- Loss of the parent's supporting assignment stops that dependent route and
  descendants requiring it, even if grant definitions remain stored.
- An owner's broader personal authority cannot substitute for the required
  parent-team source or bypass the child team's ceiling.

Legitimate root authority is a separately defined starting point. This rule for
child teams does not invent a parent for root authority or authorize arbitrary
parentless grants as a way around the hierarchy.

## Example

Related diagram: [ownership changes versus stable team-held lineage](ownership-lineage.md).

```text
Team1 holds G1: read/write, dept = FIN
  └── Team2 holds G2: read, dept = FIN AND cert = C17
        └── G2 depends on G1 through Team1's assignment
```

This is illustrative: scope keys must be supported for the same operation and
enforced on its actual data. A child with additional scope `{}` instead retains
the parent's FIN boundary without further narrowing.

## Rationale and consequences

Team hierarchy alone does not prove where assigned authority came from. Requiring
the grant source to be held by the parent team makes the authority ceiling follow
that hierarchy. The grant-to-grant relationship carries permission/scope narrowing;
the supporting assignment proves that the parent team holds the source authority.

The parent definition's existence, an assignment to another team, or an owner's
personal grant is not sufficient support. Another assignment of the same definition
must not silently replace a lost selected parent assignment. Separate administrative
authorization and the assigner's applicable source checks remain required.

This further constrains [Q-097's complete-result validation](team-grant-change-boundary.md):
for a child team, a changed grant must also remain supported by its parent team.
It does not change the existing Team1/Team2 grant files or establish their missing
team-parent and assignment-reference records.
