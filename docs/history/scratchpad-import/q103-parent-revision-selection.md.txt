## Q-103B — Resolution flows top-to-bottom: AGREED DIRECTION

The user specified that lineage resolution should flow from top to bottom.
Establish valid authority at the top of the required lineage, then evaluate
each child within the already-established effective parent boundary. Permission
selection and scope-AND constraints accumulate without amplifying authority.
This is logical evaluation order, not a mandated network-request sequence.

```text
Valid root/upstream authority
             |
             v
Effective G1 support in the actual route
             |
             v
G2: validate required binding and narrow within G1
             |
             v
G3: validate required binding and narrow within G2
```

Grant/assignment enablement, tenant, validity, and required supporting team
relationships remain mandatory at each applicable level. Ineffective or orphaned
required support cannot produce effective descendant authority. Stored grants
or assignments need not be automatically deleted or disabled by resolution.

**Core-philosophy check and rationale:** a child's ceiling is derived from
established parent authority, never independently inferred from a newer grant
definition elsewhere. This preserves recursive non-expansion. It does not
reverse Q-101's mutation ordering: affected assignment disable/removal remains
bottom-up, while resolution is top-down. No universal operation-enable ordering
or new transport contract is selected here.

Q-103A now confirms adopted, not latest published, revisions. Top-down flow
does not authorize automatic revision adoption.

## Q-103A — “Lineage latest” means currently adopted: AGREED

The user confirmed the recommended interpretation. The original clarification
and rationale below remain the explanation of the decision, not an open vote.

The user asked whether using “lineage latest” is the better shape. This is not
recorded as an approved latest-published fallback: the intended meaning needs
to be distinguished from the existing explicit-adoption rules.

**Recommendation:** interpret lineage-latest as the current valid, explicitly
adopted revisions along the actual required supporting lineage, recursively.
Not the newest published revision irrespective of what the supporting
assignments adopted. Each child's own adopted permissions and extra scope
still restrict the resulting authority.

Example: Team1's supporting assignment adopts G1 revision 1 (FIN read), while
revision 2 (FIN read/write) has been published or adopted only by TeamX. For
TeamY under Team1, lineage-latest is still Team1's adopted revision 1. After
Team1 explicitly and validly adopts revision 2, the current parent ceiling for
that route changes accordingly. This does not auto-upgrade the child's own
adopted revision or add permissions the child did not select.

**Core-philosophy check:** current adopted lineage preserves actual parent
support, non-expansion, and explicit adoption. Automatically using the newest
published revision as authority could bypass adoption and supply authority the
required parent team does not hold. It could also silently withdraw authority
when publication narrows the definition. Neither follows from Q-102.

**Original Q-103A clarification, now answered yes:** does “lineage latest” mean the latest revision actually
adopted along that supporting lineage, rather than simply the latest published
revision? No new revision schema or latest-published lookup fallback is adopted.

## Concrete example

Tenant is implied. All unrelated validity and authorization conditions hold.
For this example, Team1 has one valid supporting G1 assignment:

```text
G1 revision 1: FIN read        -- A1 --> Team1
G1 revision 2: FIN read/write  -- AX --> TeamX

G2 -- parent_grant_id --> G1
G2 -- assignment ------> TeamY -- parent team --> Team1
```

Revision 2 exists and TeamX has adopted it. Team1 has not. The existing team
ceiling already forbids TeamY obtaining write merely because TeamX has it.

## Recommendation and question

**Recommendation:** resolve G2's parent ceiling from the revision currently
adopted by its required supporting parent-team assignment: A1's revision 1,
not the latest published revision and not AX's revision 2. If A1 later explicitly
adopts revision 2 through valid authorization and boundary checks, that is the
parent revision used for this route. G2's own adopted permissions and additional
scope continue to restrict its authority; adoption does not automatically add
write to a read-only G2.

**Q-103:** should a subteam's child grant follow the revision actually adopted
by its required parent-team support, without an additional independent parent-
revision selection by the child?

## Rationale and core-philosophy check

- The parent-team ceiling is based on authority actually held, not merely
  published or held by an unrelated team.
- Parent authority stays live: no old independently pinned ceiling can bypass
  loss or narrowing of the support the child requires.
- Publishing a revision does nothing by itself. Parent adoption is explicit
  and authorized, and grant/assignment disablement still applies.
- G2's own permission selection and additional scope are not rewritten by a
  parent's adoption. A broader parent permission set alone does not add an
  unselected permission to G2.
- This reuses `parent_grant_id` and the real supporting team assignment rather
  than adding another independent selection that can diverge from parent support.

## Remaining limits

This recommendation is illustrated with one required parent-team holding.
Multiple candidate holdings of G1 at the same team, direct-human derived grants
without a parent-team context, and the resulting revision selection must be
covered before claiming a complete resolver contract. This note does not impose
a new uniqueness rule or automatically choose any broader candidate.

If parent adoption changes scope, inherited child reach may change even when
the child's additional scope stays `{}`. Complete resulting authority and
affected binding validation remain required; the permission-only example does
not waive that consequence or approve silent propagation. No new JSON field,
live runtime mutation, or lab promotion is performed by this proposal.
