## Three different relationships

- **Grant lineage:** G2 points to G1 through the proposed `parent_grant_id`.
  This supplies permission-subset and scope-AND constraints.
- **Assignment:** a grant is bound to a recipient. A1 binds G1 to Team1; AX
  binds G1 to TeamX. Separate assignments bind G-ASSIGN to Maya and G2 to Nutan.
- **Membership:** Maya is a human member of Team1 and TeamX. These relationships
  make their valid assigned authority available to her. They are not ownership.

**No ownership grant or ownership relationship is involved in this example.**
Membership does not authorize grant assignment; the separate G-ASSIGN grant
supplies that administrative permission. No team-parent relationship is assumed
between Team1 and TeamX. Nutan receives G2 directly, not through team membership.

## The grant definitions

All examples are hypothetical, not changes to existing scratch records. Tenant
is the mandatory implied boundary. These are versioned grant definitions, not
complete assignment, membership, lifecycle, or revision contracts.

G-ASSIGN, assigned to Maya, authorizes assignment creation tenant-wide:

```json
{
  "version": "1",
  "id": "G-ASSIGN",
  "permissions": ["auth:assignment::create"],
  "scope": {}
}
```

G1 is the same reusable definition assigned to both Team1 and TeamX:

```json
{
  "version": "1",
  "id": "G1",
  "permissions": [
    "hrms:payroll:payslip::read",
    "hrms:payroll:payslip::write"
  ],
  "scope": {"dept": "FIN"}
}
```

G2 is assigned directly to Nutan. Its parent reference is the shape under
discussion, not a newly approved complete dependent-grant contract:

```json
{
  "version": "1",
  "id": "G2",
  "parent_grant_id": "G1",
  "permissions": ["hrms:payroll:payslip::read"],
  "scope": {}
}
```

G2 selects read from G1. Its additional scope `{}` retains G1's FIN boundary:
effective scope is FIN AND no additional restriction, not tenant-wide access.

## Before and after

Assume all illustrated remaining records, revisions, tenant constraints, and
required upstream support are valid. At issuance Maya has administrative
authority from G-ASSIGN and source authority from G1 through her memberships.
These are separate checks under Q-100, not authority obtained by being an owner.

The hypothetical change removes **A1, the G1 assignment to Team1**, and nothing
else. It does not delete G1 or remove Maya's membership in either team.

| Relationship | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| G-ASSIGN assigned to Maya | Present | Unchanged |
| A1: G1 assigned to Team1 | Present | Removed |
| AX: G1 assigned to TeamX | Present | Unchanged |
| Maya is a member of Team1 | Present | Unchanged; no G1 access through A1 now |
| Maya is a member of TeamX | Present | Unchanged; still supplies G1 |
| G2's parent grant is G1 | Present | Unchanged |
| G2 assigned directly to Nutan | Present | Unchanged stored assignment |

## What the graph does and does not decide

The graph establishes that G1 still has a valid assignment to TeamX, Maya still
has G1 through that membership, and G2's definition remains a permission/scope
subset of G1. None of those facts alone chooses the continuing support policy
for Nutan's direct assignment.

- **Grant-parent-only interpretation:** if G1 remains valid under the chosen
  rule and no dependency on A1 is required, removal of A1 alone does not break
  G2's declared parent link. Nutan's route can remain usable, subject to every
  other required check. This is the interpretation currently being considered.
- **Specific-assignment interpretation:** if Nutan's route was explicitly
  required to depend on A1, its loss stops that route. The pictured
  `parent_grant_id: G1` does not encode that extra dependency.

These are alternative meanings to settle, not two simultaneously adopted rules.
The SVG intentionally does not draw a G2-to-A1 dependency that the proposed
records do not declare. It also does not draw Maya as Nutan's owner, proxy
principal, or automatic continuing dependency merely because she issued G2.

## Rationale

Separating edge types prevents confusing membership with ownership, assignment
with grant lineage, or issuance-time authority with continuing support. The
example exposes the actual decision: whether losing one assignment of the
parent grant must stop the child, even while the parent grant remains valid
elsewhere. We should settle that meaning before deciding whether another stored
reference is necessary.

This illustration does not decide how a grant becomes globally valid/orphaned,
whether support may automatically switch, or whether deletion and recreation
restore a dependent route. Existing handbook rules about selected support are
not deprecated by drawing the alternative. No live access, earlier scratch
grant, ownership list, membership record, or lab file is modified.
