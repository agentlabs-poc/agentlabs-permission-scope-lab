# Q-101 — Parent-grant lineage with explicit withdrawal and resumption

**PINNED — agreed discussion rules, scratchpad only.** The user asked to frame
and pin the parent-grant-only direction after clarifying direct-human and
subteam cases. This is not promotion into the frozen lab or a claim that every
lifecycle, reuse, and wire-contract detail is complete.

**Latest structural clarification — Q-101E-3:** relevant bindings may be
**removed or explicitly disabled** before a parent change/removal. The earlier
removal-only wording in Q-101E-1/E-2 is superseded and retained as history below.
Assignment re-enablement validates the current relationships and authority; it
does not restore the old structural assumptions. Bottom-up ordering remains.

## Q-101A–C — Agreed clarification: assignment, enablement, and effectiveness

**Recorded on the user's instruction. These rules supersede the earlier open
disable-granularity and descendant-resumption questions preserved below.**

| Concept | Agreed meaning |
|---|---|
| Assignment | Connects a grant to a recipient. Assignment alone does not mean enabled or effective authority. |
| Grant enablement | Grant-wide, not recipient-specific. A disabled grant supplies no authority through any assignment. If grant enablement cannot pass its required validation, it remains disabled for everyone. |
| Effective authority | Requires enabled, valid grant lineage and the relevant valid assignment and support. An enabled grant can be ineffective because required upstream authority is disabled. |

### Q-101A — Shared grant disablement

If the same G2 is assigned to TeamY and directly to Nutan, explicitly disabling
G2 stops authority supplied by G2 for both. Their assignment records remain;
other valid grants are unaffected. An assignment cannot override disablement.
This is not a new status on a scope selector: it describes the grant's scoped
authority becoming ineffective.

Removing A1 alone in the original direct-human case is a different operation
from disabling shared G2. The latter deliberately affects every G2 recipient.

### Q-101B — Shared grant enablement

For the combined example, TeamY is under Team1 and requires G1 support at
Team1. If that required support is still missing, enabling shared G2 fails;
G2 remains disabled for everyone, including Nutan. There is no partial grant
enablement for Nutan while ignoring TeamY's required support. Assignment and
grant enablement remain separate controls; assigning G2 does not enable it.

### Q-101C — Descendant effectiveness is not stored disablement

G3 derives from G2. Assume all other required grants, assignments, relationships,
and boundaries remain valid:

```text
Operation                 G2 state       G3 state       G3 authority
Disable G2                Disabled       Enabled        Ineffective
Validated enable of G2    Enabled        Enabled        Effective again
```

Disabling G2 does not automatically write a disabled state to G3. When G2 is
validly enabled, a still-enabled G3 resumes without its own enable operation.
If G3 was explicitly disabled, it stays disabled and needs explicit validated
enablement. Resumption never overrides another invalid dependency or boundary.

### Supporting group-assignment removal is the protected operation

```text
G1 -- A1 assignment --> Team1
                         | parent of
G2 ----- assignment --> TeamY
G2 -- parent_grant_id --> G1
G3 -- parent_grant_id --> G2

Remove A1 while dependent G2 is enabled --> REJECT
Explicitly disable G2                  --> G2 disabled; G3 ineffective
Remove A1 after required checks        --> May proceed; child records remain
Restore valid parent support           --> G2 still disabled; G3 ineffective
Explicitly validate and enable G2      --> Still-enabled G3 can resume
```

Mere ineffectiveness is not the explicit child disablement required before
breaking its supporting group assignment. For example, temporarily disabling
G1 does not substitute for explicitly disabling dependent G2 before removing
A1. The protection concerns affected child dependencies, not unrelated direct
assignments. Multiple affected children must not escape this protection.

### Rationale

- Assignment records describe attachment, not a promise of effective access.
- Grant-wide enablement keeps reuse coherent: the same disabled grant cannot
  simultaneously supply authority to another recipient.
- Effective authority follows the valid lineage without unnecessary persistent
  status changes on every descendant.
- Breaking supporting assignments is deliberately stricter than temporary
  disablement: explicit child disablement prevents support restoration from
  silently reactivating that child.
- These rules resolve Q-101A, Q-101B, and Q-101C without introducing a
  `parent_assignment_id` or requiring every descendant to be explicitly disabled
  merely because an ancestor was disabled.

## The canonical direction for this scratch model

> A child grant identifies its parent through `parent_grant_id`. Auth derives
> the relevant team support from the recipient's team hierarchy and current
> assignments. A separate `parent_assignment_id` is not required for the cases
> established here. Parent withdrawal and child resumption are explicit,
> authorized operations—not automatic cascading state changes.

**Clarification:** “child resumption” above means re-enabling the child that was
explicitly disabled before support withdrawal. It does not prohibit a
still-enabled descendant becoming effective again after its parent is validly
enabled, as settled in Q-101C.

“Parent grant ID is enough” means enough as the **explicit grant-lineage link**
under these rules. It does not mean enough as the entire evaluation input.
Auth still needs trusted tenant context, grant definitions, assignments, team
hierarchy, relevant human/delegation relationships, validity, and selected revisions.

## Keep the relationships separate

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Grant lineage | G2 derives its permission/scope ceiling from G1. |
| Assignment | A grant is attached to a human or team. Assignments remain separate records; their IDs may still identify them for CRUD. |
| Team hierarchy | TeamY is a child of Team1; TeamY's authority cannot escape Team1's authority. |
| Membership | A human receives a team's valid assigned business authority. |
| Ownership/administration | Separately granted permission to administer teams or authority; membership is not ownership. |

Omitting a parent-assignment reference does not remove assignments or their
administration. An ownership grant does not become a business source merely
because it permits administration. Department scope is not team membership.

## Grant examples

Tenant is the mandatory implied boundary. These definitions show permission and
scope lineage; they do not settle where every lifecycle field is stored.

Parent G1:

```json
{
  "version": "1",
  "id": "G1",
  "permissions": [
    "hrms:payroll:payslip::read",
    "hrms:payroll:payslip::write"
  ],
  "scope": {"dept": "FIN"}
}
```

Child G2:

```json
{
  "version": "1",
  "id": "G2",
  "parent_grant_id": "G1",
  "permissions": ["hrms:payroll:payslip::read"],
  "scope": {}
}
```

G2's permissions must be a subset of G1's effective permissions. Its effective
scope is G1's effective scope AND G2's additional constraints. Here `{}` keeps
FIN unchanged. It does not mean tenant-wide effective authority. Parent scope
cannot be removed or replaced; conflicting values cannot authorize wider reach.

## Direct-human case: Nutan

G1 is assigned to Team1 and TeamX. Maya is a member of both teams and has a
separate administrative grant permitting assignment. She assigns derived G2
directly to Nutan. No ownership grant is involved.

Removing A1, Team1's assignment of G1, does **not by itself affect Nutan's direct
G2 assignment**. G2 derives from G1; it is not implicitly bound to A1 merely
because Maya could access G1 through Team1. In the discussed case G1 remains
valid and assigned through TeamX, and the other necessary conditions hold.

This does not make G2 independent of G1 or establish that stored parent
definitions alone always supply valid authority. It also does not make Maya a
permanent personal dependency solely because she issued the assignment. Existing
explicit human-dependent agent/service relationships are a separate case.

## Subteam case: TeamY under Team1

The same symbols below describe a separate scenario: G2 is assigned to TeamY,
which is a subteam of Team1. A1 assigns the required parent G1 to Team1.

```text
Team hierarchy:       Team1
                       └── TeamY

Grant lineage:        G2 ── parent_grant_id ──> G1
Assignments:          G1 ── A1 ──> Team1
                      G2 ────────> TeamY
```

For TeamY, Auth must establish that Team1 validly holds the required parent
authority. G1 being assigned to an unrelated TeamX is not replacement support
for TeamY. The grant ID plus the known parent-team context identifies which
holding must be validated, without declaring an A1-ID dependency on G2.

### Required controlled sequence

| Step | Required result |
|---|---|
| Attempt to remove A1 while dependent G2 is enabled | Reject the removal. |
| Explicitly disable G2 using appropriate administrative authority | G2 no longer supplies that child authority; its records can remain. |
| Remove A1 after satisfying the dependent-disable prerequisite and other checks | Parent assignment may be removed; G2 and its child assignment are not automatically deleted. |
| Attempt to enable G2 while Team1 lacks required G1 support | Reject enablement. |
| Restore valid G1 support to Team1 | G2 stays disabled. No automatic resumption. |
| Explicitly enable G2 | Require current administrative authorization, valid parent-team support, and permission/scope boundary validation again. |

There is no mandatory automatic “on hold” stored state. The user clarified that
G2 was explicitly disabled **before** A1 could be removed. The system must not
remove A1 first and silently disable G2 afterward as the normal workflow.

Removing parent support must not leave affected enabled subteam authority
behind. The exact method of enumerating affected grants, and sequencing deeper
chains, remains an implementation-contract question, not a reason to skip checks.

Disabling is not deleting. The child grant and its assignment need not disappear.
Likewise restoring support is not permission to silently enable a disabled child.
No automatic replacement or migration of a missing parent grant is introduced.

## The four examined cases

| Case | Pinned conclusion | Limit retained |
|---|---|---|
| G1 assigned to multiple teams | A subteam validates G1 support in its own parent-team context, not anywhere G1 happens to exist. | Controlled removal must respect affected enabled child authority. |
| G2 assigned directly to Nutan | Removing A1 alone does not break Nutan's unrelated direct G2 assignment in the stated valid-G1 scenario. | Global parent validity/orphan behavior is not inferred solely from G1's existence. |
| G2 reused across teams with different parents | Permission/scope lineage stays G2 → G1; required team support is evaluated in each recipient team's context. | **Resolved by Q-101A/B:** disabling G2 affects every assignment; failed required enablement validation leaves G2 disabled for everyone. Earlier granularity question is preserved in history below. |
| Required parent support removed and later restored | Disable G2 first, remove support, restore valid support, then explicitly validate and enable G2. | Recreating a grant definition or adopting another revision is not silently equivalent to restoring the same parent authority. |

The four cases now have a coherent governing direction. This is **not** a claim
that every reuse/lifecycle edge case or canonical schema has been finalized.

## Rationale

- Keep the stored lineage small: the parent grant identifies inherited authority,
  while the recipient's team context identifies the required team holding.
- Avoid accidentally tying Nutan's direct grant to an unrelated team assignment
  or confusing the issuer's membership with ownership or continuing dependency.
- Make parent-support withdrawal deliberate: reject unsafe removal instead of
  silently breaking enabled descendants.
- Make recovery deliberate: restoring parent support must not revive explicitly
  disabled child access without a new authorized enable operation.
- Keep assignment records for administration and retrieval without requiring a
  second lineage reference merely because assignments have IDs.

## Q-101D — Settled by the existing orphan rule

The user clarified that this case was already covered by the canonical orphan
concept; it does not require inventing a new mandatory removal prerequisite.

```text
G1 -- last valid assignment --> TeamX
G2 -- parent_grant_id -------> G1
G2 -- direct assignment -----> Nutan

Remove the last required support
    --> G2 becomes orphaned
    --> G2 and descendants requiring that support are ineffective
```

This concerns loss of required parent support, not merely an unassigned reusable
definition or a legitimate parentless root. Stored grants and assignments can
remain, but their presence cannot authorize through an orphaned lineage.
Ineffectiveness does not by itself prescribe a persistent disabled-state write.

An operation/workflow layer above canonical resolution may warn about or
prevent an operation that would create an orphan. Such prevention is an
additional safeguard, not a universal prerequisite of the orphan definition.
The earlier specifically agreed supporting-subteam-assignment removal guard
remains a distinct operation rule; Q-101D does not generalize that guard into
mandatory disable-before-removal for every orphan-producing operation.

**Rationale:** canonical resolution must refuse unsupported authority regardless
of whether a higher-layer warning or prevention rule was applied. This keeps
the authorization consequence deterministic and separates it from operational
protection against accidental orphaning. It is consistent with the existing
[Q-094 orphan definition](../agentlabs-permission-scope-lab/docs/authority-lineage.md).

### Q-101E-3 — Disabled bindings permit structural changes: AGREED CORRECTION

The user corrected the removal-only restriction: changing/removing a parent is
also possible when the relevant binding is disabled. A disabled assignment may
remain stored during the change. Its existence does not force deletion first.

| Situation | Governing result |
|---|---|
| Affected binding remains enabled | Do not change/remove its bound parent beneath it. |
| All relevant bindings have been removed or explicitly disabled, bottom-up | Parent change/removal may proceed subject to operation authorization and the applicable boundary checks. |
| Structural change finishes with an assignment retained disabled | It remains disabled; no automatic re-enablement or reassignment. |
| Request to enable that assignment | Validate current parent relationships, supporting assignments, grant enablement/validity, permission/scope boundaries, and administrative authority. |
| Current required support or relationship alignment is invalid | Reject enablement; the assignment remains disabled. |
| Current conditions are valid | Explicit assignment enablement may succeed; effective access still requires all applicable checks. |

```text
Disable affected assignments from the bottom upward
                         |
                         v
Change/remove the permitted parent relationship
                         |
                         v
Retained assignments remain disabled
                         |
                         v
Explicit enable request --> validate CURRENT binding and authority
                            valid: may enable
                          invalid: reject; remain disabled
```

Example: disable A2, the G2-to-TeamY assignment, after handling any affected
lower dependencies. Move TeamY from Team1 to TeamX. A2 remains stored disabled.
Enabling A2 must now establish G2's required parent authority at TeamX; prior
support at Team1 is not sufficient. The operation does not automatically change
G2's parent reference or copy authority from the old team.

**Rationale:** a disabled binding supplies no authority, so it can be retained
through a structural change without granting access under the new structure.
Safety depends on validating the new reality before explicit enablement, not
on requiring deletion and recreation of every binding. Disabled still differs
from absent for storage and administration; it is no longer a removal-only
structural lock. Grant-wide disablement, assignment-specific disablement, and
lineage ineffectiveness remain distinct concepts.

This corrects Q-101E-1/E-2's removal-only statements, not their dependency-aware
ordering or the obligation to inspect every affected branch. No runtime
implementation, new wire field, or automatic repair/rebinding is adopted.

### Q-101E-1 — Parent change with a subgroup-assignment guard: historical approval

The user approved parent changes with this restriction: a grant's parent cannot
change while that grant has an assignment to a subgroup. Once those subgroup
assignments are removed, the parent may change through authorized administration
and the existing complete-result boundary validation.

| Situation | Parent change |
|---|---|
| G2 has an assignment to a subgroup | Reject. |
| G2 is disabled but its subgroup assignment remains | Still reject: disablement is not removal of the assignment. |
| One subgroup assignment is removed but another remains | Still reject. |
| No subgroup assignments remain | The subgroup-assignment guard is cleared; ordinary authorization and boundary checks still apply. |

“Free to change” removes this particular structural restriction; it does not
grant unrestricted administrative authority or bypass checks on remaining
assignments, inherited boundaries, or dependent authority. Removing an
assignment must itself respect applicable dependency-removal safeguards. No
automatic removal, migration, or disabling of assignments is introduced.

**Rationale:** a subgroup's assigned grant is tied to the authority of its parent
team. Repointing the grant's parent while that subgroup assignment remains would
change its supporting lineage beneath the existing team relationship. Requiring
assignment removal makes that structural change explicit while allowing the
reusable grant to acquire a different parent afterward. This concerns changing
grant lineage, not moving a team or changing membership.

### Q-101E-2 — Dependent bindings and bottom-up assignment changes: historical approval

The user clarified that assignments and parent-child relationships are
dependent. A bound parent relationship cannot be removed or changed without
first removing the relevant assignment. Assignment removal or disablement must
start with the bottom-most affected child and proceed upward.

```text
G1 -- A1 --> Team1
 ^             ^
 | parent      | parent
G2 -- A2 --> TeamY
 ^             ^
 | parent      | parent
G3 -- A3 --> TeamZ

For a change affecting this entire dependent chain:
process A3 first --> then A2 --> then A1, only as far as needed
```

Each adjacent pair is a four-part binding: parent grant assigned to parent
team, child grant derived from that parent grant, child team related to that
parent team, and child grant assigned to child team. Neither parent link may
be rewritten beneath a remaining binding assignment. Equivalent authority at
a proposed new parent is not an exception to this structural guard.

**Removal and disablement remain distinct:** disabling an assignment withdraws
its authority route but leaves its stored binding. It therefore does not alone
satisfy the requirement to remove the assignment before changing the bound
parent relationship. Bottom-up disablement can prepare the affected chain for
removal; do not treat a disabled assignment as absent.

The traversal concerns all affected dependency branches, not unrelated direct
assignments or memberships. Check shared grant use and dependent bindings before
considering a relationship free to change. Stop at the level required by the
operation; this does not require deleting an entire hierarchy for every edit.
For example, changing the leaf TeamZ's parent requires releasing its A3 binding,
not automatically removing A2 and A1 as well.

This rule concerns **assignment** removal/disablement and structural parent
changes. It does not supersede Q-101C: disabling the **grant** G2 makes G3
ineffective without automatically writing a disabled state to G3. Nor does it
replace orphan resolution: if required support is absent, the affected lineage
cannot authorize regardless of whether a preventive operation guard ran.

**Rationale:** the order follows the direction of dependence. A supporting
assignment must not be structurally changed before the child assignments that
rely on it have been dealt with. Requiring actual assignment removal before
parent changes prevents a later enable operation from reviving an old binding
against a silently substituted parent. Existing operation authorization,
complete-result boundary validation, tenant isolation, and safe concurrency
checks remain required. No new parent-assignment reference is introduced.
